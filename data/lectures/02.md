# Lecture 02 Fine-Tuning and LoRA

## Aims

- Understand the difference between pretraining and fine-tuning, and when fine-tuning is and is not suitable
- Understand the idea behind LoRA and train a small model with LoRA in Google Colab

## Key Points

1. **Pretraining and fine-tuning**: Fine-tuning means further training a model that acquired general language capabilities during pretraining, using data suited to a particular purpose.
2. **Suitable and unsuitable uses**: Fine-tuning suits teaching output formats, tone, and behavior for specific tasks. For new facts or frequently changing information, tools or external data are often more suitable.
3. **SFT**: Train on pairs of inputs and desired outputs. Data quality and format strongly affect results.
4. **PEFT and LoRA**: Train only small low-rank matrices instead of updating all parameters. This can greatly reduce required GPU memory and storage. Main hyperparameters are rank r, learning rate, and number of epochs.
5. **Relationship to QLoRA**: QLoRA holds base-model weights quantized to 4-bit and trains only the LoRA adapters on top. It reduces weight memory but does not speed up training, and quantization error may change outputs. Since the exercise model fits on a T4, we train with LoRA and investigate the differences when loading in 4-bit in the advanced section.
6. **Beware of overfitting**: Check performance on validation data not used for training.
